Identity verification, 2026-09-27.
Commands: go test ./internal/identity/... ./internal/diff/... -v, make accuracy

This file is the evidence for FR-006 and FR-007, the two requirements about element identity. It records
what is claimed, what carries the claim, and where the claim stops, because identity is the part of this
engine that is an inference rather than an observation and the two must not be presented as though they were
the same thing.

## Where the claim stops

Two boundaries are stated in `specs/001-frame-delta-engine/spec.md` under the second acceptance scenario of
user story 2, which makes them part of the requirement rather than gaps in it. They were written into the
specification in commit `416cad9`, before FR-006 and FR-007 were marked verified, so that the requirement and
the engine say the same thing.

1. **An area the same size as the element it replaces.** A cover whose area equals the covered element's
   footprint leaves no margin to exceed it, and there is no appearance evidence that separates "this element
   was repainted" from "this element was replaced in place". The engine reports a change of that element.
   `TestASameFootprintDisappearanceIsReportedAsChanged` pins it.

2. **A partial return whose mixture favours the element on screen.** A returning element that occupies only
   part of the changed area is recognised by comparing what the area looks like against both the element that
   left and the element that is there. Where that comparison genuinely favours the element on screen, which
   happens when the two are only a few levels apart in appearance, the change is reported against that
   element. Distinguishing it needs matching against parts of the area rather than a summary of it, which
   `research.md` R17 rejects on cost.

Both are the same kind of statement: the engine reports what the pixels support and does not invent the rest.
A consumer that needs certainty in either case supplies a region of interest tight enough to exclude the
surrounding content, which the contract allows.

## What the measurement adds

`make accuracy` scores the four corpus cases and asserts the class of every region in the three cases where
what happened is unambiguous. Relevant to identity:

- the occluded case: 3 pairs, every region's class asserted, F1 1.0000
- the moving case: 11 pairs, every region's class asserted, F1 1.0000
- the noise case: 119 pairs, nothing reported on any of them, which is where a false removal would show

Zero false removals across all four cases, which is NFR-005 and the reason the two boundaries above are
tolerated rather than traded away: refusing a removal costs a missed cover, and making one costs a handle
that vanishes from under a consumer.

## What this file does not show

The identity rules are scored through generated cases and unit tests, not against a real application with a
real element inventory. `docs/vv/evidence/demo-2026-09-27.txt` records the engine's output on real rendered
screen pixels, where it isolated a text field's digits, a status line's words and a clock's seconds as
separate regions with stable handles across frames, but that is a sanity check rather than a scored result:
the fixture provides no ground truth for identity.
