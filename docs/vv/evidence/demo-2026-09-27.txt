Pipeline demonstration, 2026-09-27.
Commands: make capture (real frames), make demo, make demo-generated.

The pipeline is capture, engine, stub detector. Capture reads screen frames in order, the engine turns each
frame into a delta document, and the stub detector turns each document into a decision: what a planner would
do next. The detector is deliberately trivial, because the number being measured belongs to the pipeline
rather than to the planning.

The fixture. The capture source is tools/demo/capture/legacy-form.html, a page that imitates a legacy
desktop form: a table layout, a raised title bar, inset fields, a status line with a clock, and a ledger
list. It is rendered by a real browser and captured with the DevTools screenshot path, so the material is
real screen pixels with real antialiasing and real widget edges rather than the flat panels of the generated
corpus. It is not a line of business application, and this file says so rather than implying otherwise: the
project has no access to one, and what the criterion needs is real screenshots, which this is. Every captured
frame advances the clock, so every frame carries something to decide about.

Real screenshots, 1280x720, eight frames replayed twenty times:

  frames processed        160
  elapsed                 413 ms
  frames per second       387.5
  decisions               119
  decisions per second    288.2

Generated frames, 1920x1080, two hundred frames:

  frames processed        200
  elapsed                 1.329 s
  frames per second       150.5
  decisions               144
  decisions per second    108.4

Of the 160 real frames, 119 produced a decision. That is the honest part of the number: the first frame of
each replay has no predecessor and reports nothing, and several frames change only the ledger list, which is
outside the configured region of interest. A pipeline can be fast and still make fewer decisions than frames,
and reporting only the frame rate would hide that.

Requirement this measures:
  SC-004  a demonstration pipeline of capture, engine and a stub detector sustains at least 20 decisions per
          second wall clock on real desktop screenshots
          met: 288.2 decisions per second on real 1280x720 screenshots, and 108.4 on generated 1080p frames.
          The limitation is the application rather than the rate: the screenshots are real renders of a
          fixture that imitates a legacy form, not a line of business application, which this project has no
          access to.

What the engine reported on the real pixels, as a sanity check rather than as a scored result. Over the eight
captured frames it reported forty regions, of which twenty four were changes, four additions and twelve
movements. Mapping the pixel coordinates back to the fixture shows it found the fixtures' changed text runs
and nothing invented:

  (172, 82, 35, 14)   the amount field's digits, magnitude 0.43
  (204, 82, 12, 14)   the remainder of the amount field, magnitude 0.54
  (8, 701, 52, 18)    the status line's words, magnitude 0.35
  (1261, 702, 13, 14) the clock's seconds, magnitude 0.32

That is the answer to the threat this project was most exposed to: the corpus is synthetic, and the engine
had only ever been scored on flat panels. On real antialiased text it isolates individual glyph runs with
sensible bounds, and the clock in the corner is found as a small region rather than smearing into the status
line. It is not an accuracy score, which needs ground truth the fixture does not provide, and it is recorded
as the sanity check it is.
