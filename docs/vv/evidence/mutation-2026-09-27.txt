## What the score means, and what the two remaining entries are

Thirteen of the fourteen applied mutations are killed: thirteen rules the requirements name have a test that
fails when the rule is wrong.

The one survivor is the pixel word size, an equivalent mutant. The comparison skips a row in words of eight
bytes once it knows the row changed, and it only skips a word when the two words are equal, so a smaller word
skips less and finds the same pixels. The mutation changes how much work the comparison does and never what it
reports, which is why no test can distinguish it and why none was written to.

Two of the fourteen rows in earlier runs were inconclusive rather than killed, and both were mistakes in this
list rather than gaps in the suite. A search string containing "||" split into the wrong fields on the separator
and produced a malformed edit, and a two character separator did not work either because IFS is a set of
characters rather than a string. Both produced a package that no longer built, both were reported as
inconclusive rather than as kills, and neither was visible by reading the script: the inconclusive row is what
surfaced them. That is the category doing the job it was added for.

A third mistake is worth recording because it is the same failure this project keeps finding. A test written for
the first tie-breaker mutation used a pair of regions that differed in class and in area at once, so each
tie-breaker decided the pair on its own and neither mutation could be seen through it. Splitting the pair into
one case per tie-breaker killed both. A test that passes for two reasons is a test that proves neither.
